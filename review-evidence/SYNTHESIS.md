### 2. Domain significance 2*/overall 2* versus provisional 3*

The domain reviewer gives the strongest lower-grade case: although the characterization is genuine, its motivating use remains generic, no substantial influence on economic theory or practice is demonstrated, and equivalent minimax-risk formulations have not been exhaustively excluded. EIC, Methodology, Perspective and DA offer the contrary substantive case: a complete necessary-and-sufficient characterization across arbitrary finite scalar support patterns is qualitatively stronger than a support-restricted test, with an attained coefficient theory, finiteness criterion, positive sparse case, and two distinct pairwise-failure mechanisms.

The disagreement is about **the significance of the mathematical classification itself**, not about proof correctness and not about adding the component grades. Domain expertise deserves weight, but a prospective theory assessment need not wait for uptake or empirical deployment. The central question is whether the stated result changes what can be established within a meaningful research problem. Here it does: all finite scalar support patterns admit a finite, explicit convex characterization, including the regime where every binary restriction can mislead. The sparse family shows how simultaneous constraints supply previously missing information, while the vector obstruction delimits the scalar mechanism even under full support.

**Resolution: provisional 3* overall, with genuine 2*/3* uncertainty.** This is a departure from Domain’s conservative central grade, not from its factual findings: it agrees the characterization is substantive and finds no subsuming theorem. I give the classification’s intrinsic specialist significance more weight than demonstrated downstream influence, without treating absent downstream results as irrelevant. This is not a four-to-one vote. Methodology’s competence primarily supports rigour; it is not treated as authority settling economic significance. Domain’s prior involvement reduces claims of external independence but is not used to dismiss its lower rating.

The outcome is not a confident “solid 3*.” A specialist could reasonably regard the result as a narrow consequence of standard minimax and one-dimensional interpolation, or identify a prior equivalent. Those possibilities remain materially open. They justify the uncertainty range and bar stronger publicity, not an automatic deduction to 2* in the absence of such evidence.

### 3. What changed from the earlier 2* assessment

The old assessment is neither binding nor erased. Its strongest limitation—full-support-only certification—has been removed by new mathematics. The revision now characterizes arbitrary support rather than only displaying a counterexample outside its domain. It also proves coefficient finiteness/attainment and supplies a separate vector obstruction with a categorical realization. These are substantive changes, not a longer introduction or more tests. ## Editorial decision rationale

The paper’s main achievement is a precise bridge between an infinite family of posterior inequalities and finitely many simultaneous completion systems. Classical minimax proves that universal variance domination entails common row-budget completions; scalar interpolation proves that completions at the observed source means suffice everywhere. This combination covers arbitrary finite support, handles repeated means and zero cells, and gives the sharp coefficient an attained convex formulation when finite. The full-support formula is recovered transparently. The sparse examples and full-support vector obstruction explain why neither separate binary restrictions nor a naive vector extension captures the general phenomenon.

The methodology report supplies a detailed analytic audit and eight fresh normal/optimized executions, correctly described as repeated checks of supplied code rather than eight independent implementations. The DA finds no critical or major correctness objection. Domain, EIC and Perspective identify publication-quality refinements but no claim requiring a mathematical repair. The manuscript already limits its algorithmic, statistical and economic implications appropriately.

Acceptance is therefore warranted for the stated specialist contribution. The assessment of international excellence is more uncertain than correctness: the mathematical classification provides a defensible 3* case, but its historical relationship to general minimax-risk and seminorm-extension results and its disciplinary significance remain incompletely resolved. That is why the editorial recommendation can be affirmative while the star assessment remains provisional and qualified.

## Strongest unresolved objection

The finite-knot characterization may be an elegant, model-specific application of established minimax quadratic-risk and scalar extension principles, with little consequence beyond the exact screening task. A general prior theorem may already imply it under another formulation. The vector table is a useful obstruction but not a positive vector theory. Exact real convex feasibility also does not deliver an efficient exact decision procedure at degenerate or irrational boundaries.

The manuscript responds adequately at its current claim level: it credits the established ingredients, identifies the candidate-specific finite scalar-knot step, demonstrates both positive and negative sparse applications, and refuses unsupported solver or complexity claims. What it does not supply is an exhaustive historical exclusion or a major downstream theory. The highest-value confidence improvement would be a genuinely development-independent specialist comparison of that exact equivalence, not another unanchored grading round.
